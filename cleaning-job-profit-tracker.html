JOB TRACKING · GUIDE 03

Track profit on each cleaning job

A simple method for comparing estimates with actual costs so that repeated pricing mistakes are easier to notice.

Record the estimate

Save the original quoted amount, anticipated cleaner-hours, expected material costs, travel time, scope and target margin.

Log actual job costs

After each job, write down actual hours worked, labor-related expenses, supplies, travel and allocated overhead. Include rework and customer-requested extras.

Compare expected and actual contribution

For example, if you charged $250 and originally expected costs of $160, you expected $90 in contribution. If actual costs reached $195, contribution falls to $55 before taxes and other excluded expenses.

Look for patterns

If a category of jobs regularly takes an hour longer than expected, update the quoting assumptions. One unusual job shouldn't automatically determine future rates.

Protect customer information

Use anonymized job identifiers where possible and limit access to your customer records. The free calculator requires no client details.

Test a quote ↗

Contribution is not identical to take-home profit. This guide is for education and operational planning.
