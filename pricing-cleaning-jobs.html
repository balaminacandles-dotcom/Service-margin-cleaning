CLEANING PRICING · GUIDE 01

How to price a cleaning job

A practical framework for calculating your service costs, choosing a margin target and checking your proposed quote.

1. Estimate the labor required

Determine how many cleaners will work and how many hours each will need. Include actual worker wages and additional employer costs when relevant. A $20 hourly wage may cost more once payroll taxes and other labor-related expenses are included.

2. Add non-cleaning costs

Account for supplies, transport, paid setup time, insurance, administrative effort and a fair share of overhead. Work from actual records when possible; avoid treating all revenue above wages as profit.

3. Choose the margin deliberately

If a job costs $210 to deliver and you want a 30% contribution margin, the calculation is $210 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = $300. A 30% markup on $210 would instead be $273, which is a different metric.

4. Check the proposed price

Compare the quote against customer scope and the quality and service your team can provide. Avoid treating a mathematical target as evidence that customers will accept a particular price.

5. Review after the clean

Record actual hours and job costs. The difference between quoted and actual expenses can reveal underestimated work and underpriced extras.
