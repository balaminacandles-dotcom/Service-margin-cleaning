RECURRING SERVICE · GUIDE 02

How to price recurring cleaning

Avoid discounting weekly or biweekly visits without checking what each completed clean actually costs.

Frequency changes the work

A weekly clean may take less time than a monthly clean, but the travel, setup, scheduling, and administrative steps can remain substantial. Estimate each visit type separately.

Model the economics of each visit

Start with labor, supplies, travel and an overhead allocation per visit. Apply any recurring-client discount after you've calculated the baseline cost and margin—not before.

Check example scenarios

Suppose your standard quote is $180 and estimated cost per visit is $125. Your modeled contribution is $55. A 10% discount makes the price $162 and modeled contribution $37 before taxes and any unmodeled fees. This is why a small headline discount can create a larger reduction in contribution.

Include explicit scope expectations

State what is included, expected visit length, frequency, add-ons, cancellations and how you handle significantly changed conditions. Clear scopes help prevent work from silently expanding.

Keep reviewing

Recheck pricing when labor expenses, travel time, supplies or customers' cleaning requirements change.
